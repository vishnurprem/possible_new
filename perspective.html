Perspective

AI accelerates intelligence. Leaders create conviction.

Every leadership team right now is pouring budget and attention into AI. Far fewer are investing the time to align leaders, build belief, and reinforce the behaviors that matter.
20 years
in the room.
5 observations
01

AI can tell you the moves you need to make. But it can't make your team believe or inspire them to act.

You can work through macro forces to bold moves. AI is genuinely useful here. But the thing that derails strategy isn't bad logic. It's unsurfaced disagreement and unresolved feelings. Teams nod along to a deck and unravel it behind closed doors. Belief isn't generated. It's built, in a room, through tension that gets named and debated instead of avoided.

02

AI can draft a beautiful story. It can't tell you if it's the right one.

A fluent narrative is easy to produce now. What's hard, and what AI structurally can't do, is know whether that narrative is true for this audience, whether it resolves the real tension underneath it, or whether it's just good-enough prose standing in for clarity you haven't actually reached yet.

03

AI can tell you what needs to change. It can't make people embrace the new.

The instinct under pressure is to reach for the shortest intervention. The 60-minute module, the read-only tool. But that's not how learning or change works. What actually shifts behavior is vulnerability and accountability between real people. That can't be shortened to a prompt or solved with a PDF.

04

AI can make work more productive. It can't make relationships more effective.

Speed and output have never been the bottleneck for most leadership teams. Trust has. Alignment has. The willingness to have the hard conversation that everyone's been avoiding. AI makes it faster to produce things. It doesn't make it easier to be honest with each other. And that's still where the real work lives.

05

AI can build you a well-timed agenda. It can't design an offsite or gathering worth showing up for.

Building a scheduled event with slotted speakers isn't hard. What is difficult is engineering the right sequence of experiences and conversations to foster shared conviction and culture. This is how you go from transmitting information to sparking a movement.
